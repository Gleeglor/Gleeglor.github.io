Protocol Kardashev

2024-2026 Dev build

You crash. You dig in. Then you expand hard. The long goal is climbing the Kardashev scale by building machines with your own intelligence. This page covers an early development build: playable, unfinished, and still changing.

Not a cozy little outpost

This is not the fantasy of a quiet colony that settles in and tends a garden. You wake up after a failed probe landing with cracked systems, short vision, and scrap in the dirt. The job is to take ground, grow influence, and keep pushing outward.

Problems show up constantly. Power fails. Fog hides the crust. Research gates sit between you and the next tool. The game keeps handing you tools to answer those problems in more than one way, so the climb stays yours to figure out.

Climb the scale

The north star is civilization scale. Dirt and scrap feed factories. Factories feed ships. Ships stretch toward ring slices, orbital rings, interplanetary stations, and star rings that can close into ring worlds. Planets stop being scenery and start looking like parts you might move someday.

That ladder is huge. Early play stays close to the crash hub on purpose. You still dig, claim, research, and build, but every loop is meant to point at bigger machines and harder reach.

A circle of sight in the mist

You start under soft circular fog. What you have not earned stays unread. Outside the vision dome, the world does not hand you free intel on ores and structures.

Tile probe is the close read: stone, ore, debris, empty air. Processes is the wide read: the scrap-to-stock graph that shows whether invent hubs and scrap processors are actually moving material. Both sit as panels you can drag while the planet keeps turning underneath.

Tools for the next problem

Early tools stay dark on purpose. Until mycelium makes sense, the growth network stays out of reach and construction without the right drones stays blocked. Research is the permission slip for the next layer of the planet.

Past that gate, a physics route sketches ore mining, smelting, and fabrication. Some of that climb already shapes what you can build. Some of it is still arriving. Either way, understanding is the gate, not a random drip of unlocks.

Pipelines you can watch

If a building is working, the overview should show the work. Buffers, bot use, power draw, and yields belong on the panel you already opened, so the sim never feels like a black box humming behind a static card.

Build the machines yourself

Construction turns landers and vessels into assemblies on a grid. Blocks connect, rotate, and carry systems. Hull plates are denser than frames. Thrusters add real push. Ghost placement tints green when a piece can attach and red when it cannot.

You can save full hull designs, or smaller blueprints to reuse later. When a new run starts, the built-in lander sits at the top of the picker, with custom landers waiting underneath if you have made any. The point is not picking a preset. The point is designing the next machine with your own head.

Looking farther out, the same ideas stretch toward ring slices, orbital rings, interplanetary stations, and star rings. Each step up that ladder is enormous. The long fantasy stays huge even while the early crash site stays small enough to hold in your hands.

Reading a single pixel

Inside vision, the probe is where the next dig or build decision happens. Outside vision, it stays quiet on purpose. The map only tells you what you have earned the right to know.

How it gets made

Most of the game is drafted with AI help. That is not the whole story. Every piece still goes through me, Nick, before it counts. Creative direction especially. If the tone, the fantasy, or the next goal feels off, I send it back.

So the build moves fast, but it stays mine. An early development workshop aimed at aggressive expansion, smarter machines, and a long climb up the Kardashev ladder.
